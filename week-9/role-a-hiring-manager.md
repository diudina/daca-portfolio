## Role A — Hiring Manager View: CV & LinkedIn

UrbanStyle is a fast-growing retail and e-commerce company, so the hiring manager should look for candidates who can do more than use analytical tools. A strong candidate should be able to work with different types of business data, communicate findings clearly and turn analysis into useful decisions.

### What I would look at first

When screening a CV, I would first try to answer five questions:

- Is it immediately clear that this person is relevant for a Data Analyst role?
- Do they have evidence of using SQL and Python on real analytical problems?
- Can I see business outcomes, not just a list of responsibilities?
- Have they worked with data that is relevant to a retail, e-commerce or customer-focused business?
- Is there a portfolio or GitHub where I can verify the work?

Because UrbanStyle is a relatively small and fast-growing company, I would also look for signs that the candidate is **versatile and independent** rather than specialised in only one narrow analytical task.

### What makes a strong CV for UrbanStyle

A strong CV should connect technical skills to business problems.

For example, instead of:

> Used Python and SQL for data analysis.

A stronger description would be:

> Analysed customer and sales data using SQL and Python, identified a high-value customer segment and translated the findings into retention recommendations.

Useful evidence would include:

- sales or revenue analysis;
- customer segmentation or retention analysis;
- KPI and performance reporting;
- e-commerce or retail analytics;
- dashboard development;
- automation of repetitive reporting or data-processing tasks;
- recommendations that led to a measurable business outcome.

Results should be quantified where possible: revenue, percentage change, customers affected, records analysed or hours of manual work saved.

### LinkedIn — what is worth featuring

The Featured section should show the candidate's **strongest evidence**, not simply the largest number of projects.

For UrbanStyle, particularly relevant projects would be:

**1. Sales or retail performance analysis**  
Shows that the candidate can understand revenue, trends, locations, products and business KPIs.

**2. Customer segmentation or retention project**  
Shows the ability to move from customer-level data to a business recommendation.

**3. Automated analytics pipeline**  
Shows Python skills, independence and the ability to make analytics repeatable rather than relying on manual work.

**4. Business dashboard**  
Shows the ability to communicate data clearly to managers who may not be technical.

A strong featured project should make it easy to understand:

**Business question → Analysis → Result → Recommendation**

### Green flags

**1. Business impact is visible**

The candidate describes what changed because of the analysis — for example increased revenue opportunity, cost reduction, better targeting or time saved.

**2. Technical skills are supported by evidence**

SQL, Python, visualisation and other tools appear inside real projects rather than only in a Skills section.

**3. The candidate demonstrates end-to-end ownership**

They can take a business question, work with the data, analyse it, communicate the result and make a recommendation. This is particularly valuable in a small, fast-growing company where an analyst may need to work across several areas.

### Red flags

**1. Tool-heavy but outcome-light CV**

The candidate lists SQL, Python, Power BI, Tableau and many other technologies, but it is difficult to understand what they actually achieved with them.

**2. Projects stop at the analysis**

The candidate shows queries, charts or dashboards but does not explain the business question, finding or recommendation. For UrbanStyle, analysis should help someone make a decision.

### Additional consideration for UrbanStyle

Because the company works across several locations and is also hiring remotely, the CV and LinkedIn profile should contain some evidence of clear communication, collaboration and independent work.

For remote candidates especially, it is useful to see examples of:

- documented work;
- collaboration through shared tools or GitHub;
- presenting findings to stakeholders;
- working independently with a clearly defined outcome.

### Main hiring-manager principle

For UrbanStyle, I would prioritise **evidence over claims**.

The strongest candidate is not the person who lists the most tools. It is the person whose CV and LinkedIn make it easy to see:

**I can work with the data → I can find something useful → I can explain why it matters → I can help the business decide what to do next.**
